AQA GCSE History (8145) Question Stems & Structure Guide

Exact timing, Assessment Objectives, and paragraph formulas for every paper.

2 Papers • 2 Hours Each • 168 Total Marks
Paper & Question Typical AQA Stem Marks & Time AOs Winning Paragraph Structure
1A Q1 (Interpretations) How does Interpretation B differ from Interpretation A about...? 4 marks • 5m AO4 Quote/paraphrase View B + contrast directly with View A. Focus strictly on content, not provenance.
1A Q2 (Interpretations) Why might the authors of Interpretations A and B have a different interpretation...? 4 marks • 5m AO4 Analyse provenance (who, when, purpose, audience) for both A and B and link to historical context.
1A Q3 (Interpretations) Which interpretation gives the more convincing opinion about...? 8 marks • 10m AO4 Para 1: Test A against contextual knowledge (accurate vs. exaggerated). Para 2: Test B. Para 3: Clear comparative judgement.
1A Q4 (Describe) Describe two problems / features / ways in which... 4 marks • 5m AO1 2 crisp points, each supported by 1–2 specific historical facts, dates, acts, or statistics. No introduction needed.
1A Q5 (Consequence/Change) In what ways were the lives of... affected by...? 8 marks • 10m AO1, AO2 2–3 PEEL paragraphs showing change over time or impact on different social groups (e.g., rich vs. poor, women, African Americans).
1A Q6 (Two-Bullet Essay) Which of the following was the more important reason why...? (2 bullets) 12 marks • 15m AO1, AO2 Para 1: Bullet 1 + specific evidence + link to question. Para 2: Bullet 2 + evidence. Conclusion: Directly weigh Bullet 1 vs. Bullet 2.
1B Q1 (Source Analysis) Source A supports / opposes... How do you know? 4 marks • 5m AO3 Identify 1–2 details in the source/cartoon + explain using contextual knowledge what those details mean.
1B Q2 (Source Utility) How useful are Sources B and C to a historian studying...? 12 marks • 15m AO3 Para 1: Source B Content + Provenance (COP) + Context. Para 2: Source C Content + Provenance + Context. Conclusion: Combined utility.
1B Q3 (Write an Account) Write an account of how... became an international crisis / led to... 8 marks • 10m AO1, AO2 Chronological causal chain (Trigger → Escalation → Wider consequence). Use connective phrases ("This provoked...", "As a result...").
1B Q4 (Statement Essay) ‘Statement.’ How far do you agree with this statement? 16m + 4 SPaG • 20m AO1, AO2 Para 1: Agree with stated factor. Para 2 & 3: Counter-factors. Conclusion: Sustained, weighed judgement ("How far?").
2A Q1 (Health Source) How useful is Source A to a historian studying...? 8 marks • 10m AO3 Para 1: What Source A shows (content) + contextual accuracy. Para 2: Nature, Origin, Purpose (NOP) + limitations. Mini-conclusion.
2A Q2 & Q3 (Significance / Compare) Explain the significance of... / Explain two ways in which X and Y were similar/different. 8 marks each • 10m AO1, AO2 For Significance: Short-term impact + Long-term impact. For Similarity/Difference: 2 clear thematic comparisons across both periods.
2A Q4 (Thematic Factors Essay) Has [Factor] been the main factor in...? Explain your answer across periods. 16m + 4 SPaG • 20m AO1, AO2 3–4 factor paragraphs spanning Medieval, Renaissance, 19th-C, and Modern eras + sustained comparative conclusion.
2B Q1–Q3 (Elizabethan Depth) Q1: How convincing is Interpretation A? (8m) • Q2: Explain what was important about... (8m) • Q3: Write an account... (8m) 8 marks each • 10m AO1, AO2, AO4 Q1: Test content against Elizabethan knowledge (ignore provenance). Q2: 2 explained areas of importance. Q3: Causal narrative.
2B Q4 (2027 Historic Environment) ‘Statement about the Spanish Armada (1588).’ How far does a study of the Spanish Armada support this statement? 16m + 4 SPaG • 20m AO1, AO2 Embed specific site/battle details (English Channel, Calais fireships, Gravelines, Howard & Drake, winds) across 3 balanced paragraphs + verdict.

Paper 2A: The 7 AQA Health Factors

Use these 7 thematic lenses to structure every 16-mark (+4 SPaG) Health & the People essay:

  • 1. War: Paré's ligatures & ointments, Florence Nightingale in Crimea, X-rays/blood transfusions/plastic surgery in WWI, penicillin mass production in WWII.
  • 2. Superstition & Religion: Church preserving Galen's works & banning dissection; Islamic hospitals (Rhazes, Avicenna); flagellants during the Black Death.
  • 3. Chance: Paré running out of boiling oil (1537); Pasteur's assistant leaving chicken cholera out (1879); Fleming's stray Penicillium mould spore (1928).
  • 4. Government: 1848 & 1875 Public Health Acts, Bazalgette's London sewers, Liberal Reforms (1906–11), founding of the NHS by Aneurin Bevan (1948).
  • 5. Communication: Printing press spreading Vesalius's Fabric of the Human Body (1543); Royal Society; Lancet journal; US government funding Florey & Chain.
  • 6. Science & Technology: Microscopes (Leeuwenhoek), Koch's agar jelly & chemical dyes, X-rays (Röntgen), DNA structure (1953), MRI & keyhole surgery.
  • 7. Role of the Individual: Hippocrates, Galen, Vesalius, Harvey, Jenner, Snow, Simpson, Lister, Pasteur, Koch, Fleming, Florey & Chain.

Paper 2B: 2027 Historic Environment

Spanish Armada (1588)

AQA 7-Criteria Checklist for The English Channel & Defeat of the Spanish Armada (1588):

  • 1. Location: The narrow English Channel, Calais anchorage (lacking a deep-water Spanish port), and Gravelines off the Flemish coast.
  • 2. Function: Strategic sea lane intended as Philip II’s invasion corridor to ferry the Duke of Parma’s 27,000 troops from the Netherlands to Kent.
  • 3. Structure & Ship Design: John Hawkins’s fast, low "race-built" English galleons vs. tall, heavy Spanish galleons designed for close grappling and boarding.
  • 4. People Connected: Lord Howard of Effingham, Sir Francis Drake, John Hawkins, Philip II of Spain, Duke of Medina Sidonia, and Duke of Parma.
  • 5. Tactical Design & Armaments: English long-range culverins firing broadsides outside boarding range vs. Spanish defensive crescent formation.
  • 6. Culture, Religion & Values: Catholic Crusade backed by Pope Sixtus V vs. English Protestant survival, Tilbury speech patriotism, and the Armada Portrait.
  • 7. Key Turning-Point Events: Cadiz Raid (1587), 8 English fireships at Calais (7 Aug 1588), Battle of Gravelines (8 Aug), and the "Protestant Wind" shipwrecks.
